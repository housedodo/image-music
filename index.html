What the picture controls

Ten measurements, ten synth parameters, no sequencer. Only two of them quantise — the root and the chord quality — so the odds of two unrelated photos giving the same patch are vanishingly small.

Hue → root
Circular mean of hue, weighted by saturation, stepped round the circle of fifths at 30° per step.
Lightness → chord quality
Bright to dark: maj7♯11, maj9, add9, sus2, min9, min7, min♭9. The biggest single driver of mood.
Saturation → voice count
Grey gives a bare open fifth; vivid stacks all six notes. Colour is harmonic content.
Vertical spread → voicing
Where the light sits in the frame decides close or open voicing across octaves.
Texture → detune
Local entropy drives the three oscillators per note apart, 2¢ glassy to 18¢ choral.
Brightness → filter cutoff
One lowpass: 300 Hz at night, 7 kHz at noon.
Contrast → resonance and swell
Peaky and moving, or flat and still.
Edge density → breath rate
All that's left of rhythm — one filter cycle per 40 seconds down to per 4.5.
Blur → reverb
Soft focus goes wet and distant; sharp stays dry and close.
L/R colour → stereo width
Frames whose halves differ in saturation sit wider.

Using it in a track

Transpose before exporting — the stepper moves the root and the render follows, so you can match a pad to a song's key without re-shooting anything. The MIDI file holds the same chord as held notes, so you can re-voice it with your own instrument and keep the picture's harmony while dropping its texture. The loop is the opposite trade: it keeps the texture exactly, including the detune and the filter breath, which no MIDI file can carry. Most of the time you want both — the loop as a bed, the MIDI doubling it an octave down.
