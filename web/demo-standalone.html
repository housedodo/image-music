So — does a different angle change the music?

No, and that's engineered, not lucky

Every structural decision passes through three filters before it can touch a note. Tilt the camera and the raw numbers move a few percent; all three filters eat it.

  • QuantiseHue is continuous, key isn't. One key step is 30° of hue — a 12° tilt moves the circular hue mean by 1–3°, roughly a tenth of a step.
  • SmoothEvery feature is an exponential moving average, α = 0.15. A single odd frame contributes 15% of itself and is gone in four frames.
  • GateKey, mode and tempo need the feature to move >0.08 and hold for two frames. A wobble that crosses back never commits.

Yes — when the picture is actually different

The filters absorb noise, not signal. These changes should and do rewrite the piece, which is what makes it an instrument rather than a random generator:

  • New subjectFence to horizon: vertical energy collapses, the hits become pads. Genre-level change.
  • New lightGolden hour to blue hour moves hue far enough to walk several steps round the circle of fifths.
  • ReframingZooming until a red door fills the frame is a new photo, not a new angle — the hue mean is now the door's.
  • Hue shiftTry the +120° button above. Same structure, same rhythm, key moves four steps. Rhythm is geometry; harmony is colour.

The one genuine failure mode: a scene sitting exactly on a quantisation boundary, where the key flips back and forth. The gate's hysteresis is what stops that, and it's the first thing to widen if you ever hear it.

Application or web?

Web, and for longer than you'd think. This page is the whole stack — analysis, mapping, synthesis, sequencing — in one file with no build step, running on a phone from a link. A camera-driven version is getUserMedia plus the same code. You only need a native app for three things: background audio, low-latency MIDI hardware, or shipping it as a DAW plugin — and all three are Stage 5 concerns. What's worth splitting out early is an offline Python renderer that emits MIDI, so a musician can open the result in Ableton; it shares this page's mapping constants and nothing else.
