How a photo becomes a card

Seven steps, all in the browser, no model and no server. The whole thing is about 300 lines.

  • 01Find the subject. Median colour of the border ring is assumed to be background; pixels far from it in RGB are foreground. A majority filter fills the holes.
  • 02Crop square to the subject's bounding box plus a small margin, so the cat fills the sprite instead of the room.
  • 03Downsample to 32×32 by box-averaging — the browser's own image scaler does it.
  • 04Quantise to 6 colours with median-cut, then add a darkened outline colour and trace the silhouette.
  • 05Measure. Coverage, colour spread, edge roughness, contrast, dominant hue — the numbers that become stats.
  • 06Name and roll rarity from a 32-bit hash of the quantised sprite, so the output is deterministic: the same cat gives the same name every time, and rarity is weighted by how unusual the coat actually is.
  • 07Deduplicate with a 64-bit perceptual hash. Within 8 bits of one you own, it's the same cat — sighting count goes up instead of a new card.

The honest gap: step 01 is a background heuristic, not a cat detector. A real version runs a small on-device model (MobileNet or a YOLO-nano) to confirm "cat" and give a proper box; that's one dependency and roughly a 3 MB download, and everything downstream stays exactly as it is.

Sprites are 32×32 · palettes are 6 colours + outline · all processing local to your browser
